Guide for adults

How to practise with a child

Treat the soroban as a tool for understanding numbers rather than a speed test. Short, calm sessions work well.

1. Start with one rod

Use numbers 0–9. Lower beads are worth 1 and the upper bead is worth 5. Ask the child to set 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 and 9.

2. Say each move aloud

Encourage explanations such as “I have 5 and add 3, so I make 8.” This shows whether the child understands the move.

3. Complements to 5

Remember 1 with 4 and 2 with 3. For 5 − 3, remove 5 and add back 2: −5 +2. The child should understand that 3 + 2 = 5.

4. Complements to 10

Then learn 1–9, 2–8, 3–7, 4–6 and 5–5. For 8 + 5 use +10 −5. For 13 − 7 use −10 +3.

5. Move to larger numbers gradually

Once one rod and the 5/10 complements are comfortable, practise 12, 25, 31 and 47, then simple operations.

6. Mistakes are useful

There is no timer. If an answer is wrong, return to the last correct state and inspect one rod at a time.

A 10-minute session

  • 2 minutes of free number setting,
  • 3 “Set a number” tasks,
  • 3 “Read a number” tasks,
  • 2 operations using a complement to 5 or 10,
  • one task chosen by the child.
Child privacy: the exercises do not ask for a name, account, school or public ranking. Do not encourage children to enter personal information.
